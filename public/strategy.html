Positioning & message hierarchy

Start with what the customer needs to understand, believe or notice. Product features, commercial offers and customer friction are translated into a communication hierarchy rather than a list of everything the business wants to say.

One useful promise

Choose the point that deserves the most attention instead of giving every message equal weight.

Proof before decoration

Use product truth, service evidence and customer context to make the promise believable.

Campaign systems that survive more than one format

A campaign should still feel like the same idea when it moves from Meta to Google, a homepage hero, a collection page or a motion asset. I define the idea, offer hierarchy, visual rules and reusable content modules before format production begins.

Creative testing with a reason

Testing is useful when the variants answer a question. That can mean changing the hook, proof, offer, framing, audience angle or landing-page promise—not producing ten cosmetic versions and calling it learning.

The handoff after the click matters.

Creative strategy is incomplete if the advertisement is strong but the customer arrives at a confusing page. The message, product information, trust, offer and primary action should feel like one connected conversation.
