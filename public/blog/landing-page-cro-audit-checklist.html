Landing Page CRO Audit Checklist: What to Check Before Redesigning

A weak landing page does not always need a prettier layout. It needs a clear diagnosis of intent, message, proof, friction and measurement.

Quick answer: Audit a landing page in this order: verify the traffic and tracking, check whether the first screen matches visitor intent, make the offer and next action obvious, review proof and objections, test the full mobile journey, then identify friction around the CTA. Redesign only after you can describe the problem you are trying to solve.

Start with the job of the landing page

Before reviewing typography, spacing or button colours, define what the page is supposed to achieve. A page built to sell one product has a different job from a lead-generation page, a service page or a collection landing page.

Write down three things: where the visitor came from, what they were expecting, and the primary action you want them to take. If those answers are vague, the page will be difficult to audit because there is no clear standard for success.

If the visitor arrives from paid acquisition, use the ad-to-landing-page message match checklist alongside this audit. A good page cannot fully repair a misleading or mismatched ad.

1. Verify tracking before judging the page

A conversion problem can look like a design problem when measurement is incomplete. Test the main journey yourself and confirm that the important actions are recorded at the correct moment.

  • Does the page view register once?
  • Does the primary CTA event fire only after the intended action?
  • Can you separate mobile and desktop performance?
  • Can you identify the major traffic sources or campaigns?
  • Can you trace the next step after the landing page?

If the page belongs to an ecommerce journey, the ecommerce conversion funnel analysis guide can help determine whether the real loss happens here or later.

2. Audit the first screen for clarity

The first screen should answer the visitor's immediate questions without making them decode the page.

What?What is being offered?
For whom?Is it relevant to me?
Why?Why should I care?
Next?What should I do?

A clever headline is not useful if the visitor cannot identify the product, service or outcome. Supporting copy should add useful context rather than repeat the headline in different words.

Check the first screen at a realistic mobile width. If the product, offer or CTA is pushed below a large decorative image, decide whether that hierarchy serves the visitor's task.

3. Check whether the offer is understandable

Visitors should not have to assemble the commercial offer from several sections. Make price, inclusions, eligibility, important conditions and the next step easy to understand where relevant.

For ecommerce, check whether variant choices, delivery expectations and meaningful purchase conditions are available before the shopper commits. For a service, explain what happens after the enquiry and what the person is actually requesting.

Do not confuse urgency with clarity

Countdowns, badges and repeated calls to action cannot compensate for an unclear offer. If urgency is genuine, explain it plainly. If it is not necessary, remove it and let the proposition do the work.

4. Review proof where doubt appears

Proof works best when it answers a real question. A testimonial can support credibility. Product reviews can reduce uncertainty about quality or fit. A case example can show how a service was applied. Policies can reduce concern around delivery, returns or commitment.

  • Is the proof specific enough to be useful?
  • Is it placed near the claim or decision it supports?
  • Can a visitor understand where the proof comes from?
  • Are important limitations or conditions visible?
  • Does the page avoid claims it cannot support?

Do not add a wall of logos or testimonials simply because another site has one. Ask what uncertainty the proof is meant to resolve.

5. Audit CTA friction, not just CTA design

The primary CTA should describe a sensible next action. Then test what happens after the click. A prominent button can still lead into a poor experience.

For lead generation, review the number of fields, required information, error handling and what the visitor expects after submission. For ecommerce, review variant state, cart behaviour and the handoff into checkout. The Shopify product page CRO checklist covers PDP-specific purchase friction in more detail.

6. Test the complete mobile journey

Do not evaluate mobile by shrinking a desktop browser and looking at the hero. Use the page as a visitor would.

  1. Load the page on a phone-sized viewport.
  2. Read the first screen without zooming.
  3. Open menus, accordions and selectors.
  4. Use the main CTA.
  5. Complete the form or cart step where possible.
  6. Check keyboard behaviour, validation and error messages.
  7. Look for sticky elements covering useful content.
  8. Check whether images or scripts delay interaction.

7. Remove distractions around the main decision

A landing page does not need to be empty, but every prominent element should earn its place. Competing CTAs, unrelated navigation, auto-playing media, pop-ups and excessive promotional messages can make the next action harder to identify.

Removal is not automatically an improvement. Navigation may be useful for visitors who need more context. Secondary links can help people who are not ready for the primary action. Judge elements by whether they support or interrupt the page's job.

8. Turn observations into testable problems

A useful audit does not end with a list such as “improve hero”, “add trust” and “make CTA stronger”. Translate observations into specific problems.

Weak: The hero needs improvement.

Better: Visitors from the product-specific ad land on a page whose first screen names the brand but not the advertised product or offer, so the connection to the ad is unclear.

Weak: Add more social proof.

Better: The page makes a durability claim beside the CTA but provides no nearby evidence or explanation for that claim.

A specific problem gives design, copy and testing work a purpose.

Landing page CRO audit checklist

  1. Define the page's primary visitor and conversion action.
  2. Verify analytics and conversion tracking.
  3. Check traffic-to-page intent alignment.
  4. Review first-screen clarity on mobile and desktop.
  5. Make the offer and important conditions understandable.
  6. Check whether proof answers real objections.
  7. Test the CTA and the step after it.
  8. Complete the mobile journey yourself.
  9. Review distracting or competing elements.
  10. Check obvious performance or interaction problems.
  11. Write specific problem statements.
  12. Prioritise changes by evidence, impact and effort.

When should you redesign the whole landing page?

A full redesign makes sense when the current structure prevents you from solving important communication or usability problems efficiently. It can also be reasonable when the page has accumulated conflicting sections from many campaigns or no longer reflects the actual offer.

But if the evidence points to one unclear offer, one broken mobile interaction or one mismatched campaign, solve that problem first. The goal of CRO is not to keep redesigning pages. It is to make better decisions about what should change and why.

FAQ

What should I check first in a landing page CRO audit?

Start with visitor intent and the first screen. Confirm that the page clearly shows what is being offered, who it is for, why it matters and what the visitor should do next before reviewing smaller design details.

Should I redesign a landing page if conversion rate is low?

Not automatically. Verify tracking, traffic quality, campaign-to-page alignment and where visitors drop first. Redesign when the evidence points to page communication or experience as a meaningful problem.

What is the difference between a landing page audit and a full CRO audit?

A landing page audit focuses on one destination and its role in a visitor journey. A full CRO audit can also examine acquisition context, product pages, navigation, cart, checkout, analytics and other stages that affect conversion.
